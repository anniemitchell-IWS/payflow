# User Research

## Research Objective

The goal of this research is to better understand how variable-income workers currently track, organize, and verify their earnings.

This research informs product decisions by identifying user behaviors, pain points, and opportunities for improvement.

---

# Primary User Groups

PayFlow is designed for workers whose income changes based on shifts, tips, commissions, contracts, or multiple income sources.

Primary users include:

- Restaurant servers
- Bartenders
- Nurses working variable schedules
- Contractors
- Freelancers
- Commission-based sales professionals
- Barbers and beauty professionals
- Gig workers
- Individuals working multiple jobs

---

# User Goals

Users want to:

- Understand how much they earn
- Track income over time
- Identify earning trends
- Organize financial records
- Verify income when necessary
- Reduce manual tracking

---

# Current Behaviors

Users may currently rely on:

- Payroll portals
- Employer apps
- Banking apps
- Handwritten notes
- Screenshots
- Phone calculators
- Excel or Google Sheets
- Memory

These methods often require users to combine information from multiple sources.

---

# Pain Points

Research identified several recurring challenges.

## Fragmented Information

Income information exists across multiple apps and documents.

---

## Manual Tracking

Many users manually record earnings or perform calculations themselves.

---

## Limited Visibility

Users often struggle to understand long-term earning patterns.

---

## Income Verification

Preparing proof of income can require collecting documents from several different sources.

---

# Product Assumptions

Current assumptions include:

- Users prefer one centralized platform.
- Reducing manual entry increases adoption.
- Income analytics provide meaningful value.
- Users benefit from visual earnings trends.
- Simpler documentation improves income verification.

These assumptions should be validated through future user interviews and usability testing.

---

# Research Limitations

This case study represents an independent product concept.

Additional qualitative interviews, surveys, and usability testing would strengthen future product decisions.
